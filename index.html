Section Two

The year it was built is working against you

Every home that leased in this set was built before 1960, or is a restored older home with genuine character. The 1925 property, renovated, held $2,499. A 1957 home priced correctly went in seven days.

The only home in this set built after 2000 is the one that has not leased. 1455 Catherine Street, built 2008, is 667 square feet larger than your home with a two-car garage — and it has been sitting for 102 days at $2,995.

That is the clearest ceiling in this analysis. The late-2000s band has neither the finishes renters expect from newer construction nor the character of a restored older home. If a bigger home of that vintage with two garage bays cannot move at $2,995, your home will not approach it.

Section Three

Two more things capping the number

It is the smallest home in the set

At 1,275 square feet, your home is smaller than all eight comparables. The next smallest is 1,313.

There is no garage

Paver parking against one-car and two-car garages on several competing homes. It is a small thing on paper and a real one to a renter comparing two listings.

What works in your favor is the three bedroom, two and a half bath configuration, which is strong at this size, and the 32806 location, which is genuinely desirable. Those are why the home supports $2,500 at all.

Section Five

Why $2,395

It captures a real increase

$145 a month, or $1,740 across the year. That is a 6.4% increase on residents who have been in place a year and pay on time without exception.

It is small enough that they absorb it

A resident facing a 6% increase renews. A resident facing a 12% increase starts looking — and once they start looking, you are exposed to everything in Section Four whether they move or not.

It keeps a known quantity in place

Excellent payers are worth more than the last hundred dollars. You know what you have. The next household is an unknown, placed after a vacancy, in a market where three of eight comparable homes cannot find a renter.

It leaves room to take again next year

A measured increase now, and another at the next renewal, moves the rent upward across two cycles without ever putting the occupancy at risk. The market will likely have strengthened by then as well.

If you would prefer to push further, I will prepare whatever number you decide on. I would only want you to go in knowing that a few hundred dollars of annual gain is being risked against a turnover that costs considerably more than that if the home takes more than a week to fill — and nothing in the current data suggests it would.
